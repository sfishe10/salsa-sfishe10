Stationary Basics, Part 2	instructor	setup	Station 2: Stationary Basics Pt. 2\n\n10.\tLeft Face\na.\t2 Count Move\nb.\tCount 1\ni.\tThe left toe is raised off the ground\nii.\tThe right heel is raised off the ground\niii.\tThe player is now balanced on their left heel and right toe\niv.\tThe player pivots 90 degrees to the left by pushing with their right toe\n1.\tThe knee may bend during this maneuver\nc.\tCount 2\ni.\tThe player brings the right foot forward to meet the left and is returned to the attention position\nd.\tThe upper body remains stable and in the attention position throughout this process by engaging the core\ni.\tPlayers may find it difficult to not swing their arms. Discourage this behavior by inviting players to grab their pockets or the hem of their shirts\ne.\tVerbal\ni.\t“1-2”\n11.\tRight Face\na.\t2 Count Move\nb.\tCount 1\ni.\tThe right toe is raised off the ground\nii.\tThe left heel is raised off the ground\niii.\tThe player is now balanced on their right heel and left toe\niv.\tThe player pivots 90 degrees to the right by pushing with their left toe\n1.\tThe knee may bend during this maneuver\nc.\tCount 2\ni.\tThe player brings the left foot forward to meet the right and is returned to the attention position\nd.\tThe upper body remains stable and in the attention position throughout this process by engaging the core\ne.\tVerbal\ni.\t“1-2”\n12.\tAbout Face\na.\t3 Count Move\nb.\tCount 1\ni.\tThe left foot is moved approximately 18 inches (slightly less than 8/5 step in front of the right foot, foot at a parallel angle to the right foot (step size will take some practice)\nii.\tRoll through the step so the right and left heels come off the ground such that the player is balancing on their platforms\n1.\tNOTE: Do NOT cross the right foot over the left in this count\nc.\tCount 2\ni.\tThe player pivots over their right shoulder by pushing with their left foot.\n1.\tNOTE: Encourage proper upper body form by reminding players to tighten their core and not swing their arms (see left and right face)\nd.\tCount 3\ni.\tThe left foot is brought forward to meet the right in the attention position\ne.\tVerbal\ni.\t“Len-Len-Len”\n13.\tMark Time \na.\tThe left heel is raised on the “and” of the previous beat\nb.\tThe left toe then leaves the ground and the foot moves to a zero-degree angle, straight ahead\nc.\tThe left heel then strikes the ground on count 1 and the right heel is raised and the right foot moves to be parallel to the left\nd.\tThe right heel strikes the ground on count 2 and the left heel is raised, but the platforms and toes do not leave the ground anymore, only heels and arch\ne.\tThis continues in a fluid motion for the duration of the move\nf.\tNOTE: The heel should be raised to approximately ankle bone height. Further, the move should be smooth, and the ankle bones should cross each other on the and of each beat. Be aware of swaying side to side or heavy heel strikes that make the player’s sound bumpy.\ng.\tVerbal:\ni.\t“And-1”\n14.\tHalting\na.\t2 Count move\nb.\tCount 1\ni.\tThe right foot is raised fully off the ground and planted back in the attention position\nc.\tCount 2\ni.\tThe left foot is raised fully off the ground and planted back in the attention position\nd.\tVerbal\ni.\t“Step and Close”\n15.\tSlow Turns\na.\tThese moves must also follow the mark time form\nb.\tVariable count move\nc.\tFor the given counts:\nd.\tMaintaining mark time form, rotate the body at a constant speed through the angle requested.\ne.\tExample: 4 count 90 degrees to the left\ni.\tCount 1\n1.\tInitiate turning motion by lifting full foot off the ground and replanting\nii.\tCount 2\n1.\tHit 45 degrees between start and end points\niii.\tCount 4\n1.\tReturned to attention position with body facing 45 degrees to the left of initial position \n

Beginnings of Movement	instructor	setup	Station 3: Beginnings of Movement\n16.\tForward March\na.\tWe follow the “glide step” marching form\ni.\tThe feet remain low and close to the ground\nii.\tThe knees remain mostly unbent, however some bending is allowed for comfort and maintaining the form\niii.\tThe heel strikes on each downbeat\niv.\tAfter the heel strikes, the player roles through the foot slowly transitioning the weight from heel to toe such as that on the next downbeat, the player’s entire weight is resting on the toe\nv.\tThe size of each standard 8-5 step is 22.5” from arch to arch\n1.\tThis step remains consistent across the entire five-yard interval\nb.\tThe upper body remains in the attention position throughout the entire move (the goal is to keep the upper body free from sway and bounce that affects the player’s sound)\nc.\tNOTE: Encourage players to practice balancing on the toes/ball of one foot and the heel of another. This will encourage proper form.\nd.\tVerbal\ni.\t“And one”\n17.\tLeft Flank\na.\tEquate the left flank to a left hace, but while in motion\nb.\t2 Count Move\ni.\tCount 1\n1.\tThe right foot executes a full regular glide step  and the player’s weight is shifted to their platforms. The left foot begins to leave the ground as if for another normal forward step. No cheat steps!\n2.\t\nii.\tCount And of 1\n1.\tOn the and of one, the ankle bones come together with the left foot on the inside of the turn (the leg shouldn’t swing out)\n2.\tThe player pivots on their right platform ninety degrees to the left.\niii.\tCount 2\n1.\tOn the downbeat of count 2, the left heel strikes the ground in the first step of a forward march in the new direction.\n18.\tRight Flank\na.\tEquate the right flank to a right hace, but while in motion\nb.\t2 Count Move\ni.\tCount 1\n1.\tThe right foot executes a full regular glide step  and the player’s weight is shifted to their platforms. The left foot begins to leave the ground as if for another normal forward step.\n2.\t\nii.\tCount And of 1\n1.\tOn the and of one, the ankle bones come together with the left foot on the outside of the turn (the leg shouldn’t swing out).\n2.\tThe player pivots on their right platform ninety degrees to the right.\niii.\tCount 2\n1.\tOn the downbeat of count 2, the left heel strikes the ground in the first step of a forward march in the new direction.\nc.\tNOTE: It is common for players to “swing their left foot wide.” Discourage this behavior by having players pause on the and of count 1 with their ankles together.\n19.\tTo the Rear\na.\tEquate to the rear to an about face, but in motion\nb.\t2 Count Move\ni.\tCount 1\n1.\tThe right foot executes a full regular glide step  and the player’s weight is shifted to their platforms. The left heel begins to leave the ground as if for another normal forward step.\nii.\tCount And of 1\n1.\tThe player pivots on their platforms 180 degrees over their left shoulder. \niii.\tCount 2\n1.\tThe left foot is replanted adjusting to a full-size step beginning with the heel.\n2.\tThis initiates the next “forward march” maneuver\nc.\tNOTE: The ankle bones do NOT meet on the and of 1 of this move!\n20.\tHalting\na.\t2 Count Move\ni.\tCount 1\n1.\tThe right foot executes an adjusted roll step . This roll step follows normal form, but places the right foot in the attention position\nii.\tCount 2\n1.\tThe left foot executes a second adjusted roll step to meet the right foot and return to the attention position.\nb.\tNOTE: Players may have a tendency to sway forwards and backwards as their forward momentum is halted. Combat this by instructing players to engage their cores.\nc.\tVerbal\ni.\t“Step and Close”\n

Directions of Movement	instructor	setup	Station 4: Directions of Movement\n21.\tAdjusted Step Sizes\na.\t6-5\ni.\tThis step size is larger than 8-5\nii.\tThe full form from “Forward Marching” should be maintained despite the change in step size\n1.\tPay particular attention to players who may overstep early and correct by collapsing their step size in the end. Combat this by having players complete the exercise with their eyes closed.\nb.\t12-5\ni.\tThis step size is smaller than 8-5\nii.\tThe full form from “Forward Marching” should be maintained despite the change in step size\n1.\tPay particular attention to players who may fail to lift their foot off the ground and instead “shuffle” combat this by gradually shrinking the step size down to 12-5\n22.\tBackwards March\na.\tWe follow the “backwards glide step” form\ni.\tPlayers are always up on their platforms. This means the heels are off the ground slightly, with the full weight on the balls of the feet.\n1.\tThe heels should not be far off the ground, hovering just enough for clearance.\nii.\tFor each step, the player lifts the corresponding foot and pushes backwards. The heel does \tNOT touch the ground when the foot is replanted.\niii.\tStep size is specified. However, for exercises use 8-5.\nb.\tThe upper body remains in the attention position. Encourage proper form by reminding players to engage their core.\nc.\tNOTE: Most players will under-step the 8-5. Discourage this by having players push their feet back farther especially on count 1.\n23.\tTransitioning Between Forwards and Backwards\na.\tForwards to Backwards\ni.\t1 count move\nii.\tOn the final step of the forward march, players execute a normal roll step \niii.\tOn the and in-between the last forward step and first backwards step, players come up on the platforms\niv.\tThe left foot lifts slightly and is replanted on the first count of the backwards march\nv.\tNOTE: Balance is often an issue in this maneuver, remind players to engage their core.\nb.\tBackwards to Forward\ni.\t1 count move\nii.\tOn the final step of the backwards march, players execute a normal backwards step on their platforms\niii.\tOn the and in-between the last backward step and the first forward step, players come back down to their right heel.\niv.\tThe left foot lifts slightly and the heel is replanted on the first count of the backwards march.\nv.\tNOTE: Balance is often an issue in this maneuver, remind players to engage their core. \n

Intermediate Marching Technique	instructor	setup	Station 5: Intermediate Marching Technique \n24.\tForward Left Slide\na.\tThe Forward Left Slide follows all the lower body form of the forward march command\nb.\tIn the Forward Left Slide, the feet move to the players left while the upper body remains facing forward.\nc.\tThis is accomplished through a rotation of the hips and shoulders. The shoulders remain perpendicular to front while the hips are at an approximate 45 degree angle to the front. This is essential to achieving full rotation! Work with the group on turning their hips AND shoulders to 45 degrees, then isolate the shoulder rotation continuing to 90 degrees as the hips stay at 45.\nd.\tOn the first step of a Forward Left Slide, the left foot kicks out to the players left.\n25.\tForward Right Slide\na.\tThe Forward Right Slide follows all the lower body form of the forward march command\nb.\tIn the Forward Right Slide, the feet move to the players right while the upper body remains facing forward.\nc.\tOn the first step of a Forward Left Slide, the left foot comes in front of the right and is planted to the right of the right foot.\n26.\tBackward Left Slide\na.\tThe Backward Left Slide follows all the lower body form of the backward march command\nb.\tIn the Backward Left Slide, the feet move to the players left while the upper body remains facing forward.\nc.\tOn the first step of a Backward Left Slide, the left foot kicks out to the players left and the platform is planted.\n27.\tBackward Right Slide\na.\tThe Backward Right Slide follows all the lower body form of the forward march command\nb.\tIn the Backward Right Slide, the feet move to the players right while the upper body remains facing forward.\n28.\tOn the first step of a Backward Right Slide, the left foot comes behind the right foot and the platform is planted to the right of the player’s right foot.\na.\tSlide Transitions For EVERY transition between forwards/backwards/slide marching, the right foot pivots on the and count of beat 8 in preparation for the next direction of travel, just like for flank maneuvers. Really emphasize the vocal “and” in time with the movement of the right heel\nb.\tThe left leg should never swing, it should meet the right heel on the “and” beat just like in a flank maneuver.\n\n \n

Good Luck.\n32.\tCal Poly High Step\na.\tJust Say goodbye to your legs.\n33.\tRun On\na.\tAKA Death.\n\n
